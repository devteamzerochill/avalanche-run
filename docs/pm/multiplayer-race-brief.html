Project brief for the project manager

Avalanche Run — Multiplayer race: how it works and how it earns

25 November 2026 · Based on the project's design notes, decision log, server code and database scripts · Plain-language summary, not legal advice

1. Summary

The Multiplayer race is a live online contest inside Avalanche Run. A new race starts every 5 minutes. Players pay an entry fee in Shards (the zerochill.com currency) to check in, everyone rides the same mountain at the same time, and the last rider still alive wins. The winner receives 90% of the pot; the remaining 10% is a house fee paid to one Zero Chill account, or "burned" (removed from circulation) if that account is not set. If fewer than 2 players check in, or the winner is found to be the game's own bot, the race is cancelled and every player gets their fee back. A game server replays every rider to decide the result, so a phone cannot simply claim a win. The race server runs on Colyseus Cloud (Singapore) and the Shards ledger lives in the zerochill.com database. Races are live with real Shards today; the main open items are the website's Terms update, the confirmed live fee level, and the legal and app-store work for the phone apps.

2. How a race works for a player

1. Check inPay the entry fee for the next race
2. LobbyWait on the start grid, quick chat, may still leave
3. LockCheck-in closes 8 s before the start
4. Intro4 s flyover, then 3-2-1
5. RaceLast rider alive wins
6. ResultsPodium, prize, fee; rematch

Schedule and sign-in

  • A race starts every 5 minutes, all day. Check-in for the next race is always open.
  • Racing needs a zerochill.com account. The race server checks the player's sign-in when they join, so every result belongs to a real account.
  • On the home screen the race has its own panel next to solo play, with a LIVE badge, the countdown, the pot, the number of riders and a CHECK IN button showing the fee.

Check-in, lobby and withdrawing

  • Checking in takes the entry fee from the player's Shards and holds it for that race.
  • The player then waits in the race lobby. Everyone who has checked in stands on the start grid in their own outfit, with name tags.
  • Quick chat: six fixed phrases and six emotes, shown in each player's own language. There is no free text, so nothing needs moderating. At most one line every 1.5 seconds, and only riders in the same lobby or race see it.
  • Withdrawing: until check-in locks (8 seconds before the start) a player may leave the lobby and gets the whole fee back. After the lock, they are committed.

Countdown and start

  • At the lock the server shuffles the riders into a start grid (rows of 4). The lobby clock ends, a 4-second camera flyover sweeps over the grid, then a 3-2-1 countdown starts the race.
  • The countdown takes over the game wherever the player is. Pausing is not possible during a race.

During the race

  • Everyone rides the same mountain. Riders do not collide; the others are shown as live riders in their own look. A place card shows position, riders left and the gap to the rider ahead or behind.
  • The course gets harder: from 7,000 m to 11,000 m (about 2 to 3 minutes in) the slope gets denser, with obstacle rows 30% closer and more hazards. After 190 seconds the avalanche speeds up by 0.3 m/s every second until nobody can outrun it. Races are designed to end in about 4 minutes, leaving time for results and the next check-in.
  • Last rider standing wins. Riders who go out on the same moment are ranked by distance travelled; only an exact tie on distance too splits the prize.
  • A player whose connection stops for 3 seconds, or who leaves, is out at that point, with no refund. A player who is out can watch the remaining riders.

Results and rematch

  • Results show the player's own place, a podium for the top three, the prize paid and the house fee (shown as "Fee", or "Burned" when no house account is set).
  • A Race leaders board in Ranks lists riders by wins, then Shards won. Only settled races count.
  • Rematch ("Ride it again"): between races, a player can ride the last fair race's slope against the recorded rides of its top 3. It is practice only: no fee, no prize, nothing saved. A voided race offers no rematch, and a server restart forgets the last race.

3. Money flow and monetization

How the house earns Every entry fee goes into the race's pot. When a race finishes normally, the winner (or tied winners) receive 90% of the pot and the house account receives the rest: 10%, plus any single Shard left over when a prize is split. The house does not put any Shards in; the pot is funded only by entry fees. Cancelled and voided races earn the house nothing.

Step by step

StepWhat happens to the Shards
Check inThe entry fee is taken from the player's wallet and held for the race (added to the pot). A player without enough Shards cannot check in.
Leave the lobby before the lockThe full fee goes back to the player.
Race settled (normal finish)Winner gets 90% of the pot, rounded down to whole Shards. The rest goes to the house account, or is burned if no house account is set.
Race cancelled or voidedEvery fee goes back in full. No prize, no house fee.

Every race ends in exactly one of three ways: settled (paid out), cancelled (all refunded) or voided (all refunded). Only the race server can move Shards; the player's browser or phone cannot.

Actual earnings depend on how many players race, which this brief cannot predict.

Where the house fee goes

  • The fee is paid to one Zero Chill account named in the race settings (avalanche_race_config.house_user). No player plays on it. It is separate from the race server's own login, so the server never holds Shards.
  • The house account is set on the live site (checked in the live race settings on 25 November 2026), so the fee is being collected now. Had it been unset, the fee would have been burned (nobody receives it); no Shards have been burned so far.
  • Changing the house account later only affects races settled from then on.
  • The entry fee and the prize share can be changed on the server without a game update. A race keeps the fee and share it opened with.

Can Shards be turned into real money?

According to the zerochill.com Terms (as read on 24 November 2026 for the legal check):

  • Shards have "no cash value". They cannot be cashed out, sold, transferred between players, or redeemed for money or crypto.
  • Players can buy Shards with real money (with a first-purchase doubling bonus).
  • Shards unlock items; the game's own Shards shop sells cosmetic items only (trails, a board and a hoodie), which never affect races or scores.
Why this matters Because Shards can be bought, a paid entry for a Shards prize looks like paying to win a prize of value. The legal check found this carries gambling risk in several US states, and the 10% house cut (a "rake") adds to it. It also conflicts with the Terms' line "No purchase is ever needed to enter an event or win a prize". The owner chose to keep paid entry and the house fee and to limit who may race through the Terms (see section 6).

4. Fairness and anti-cheat

  • The server decides. Each phone plays the race locally for instant controls and sends its button inputs 10 times a second. The server replays every rider's inputs with the game's own simulation and alone decides who went out, the places and the winner. Phones only send inputs.
  • Same mountain for everyone. All riders share the same course and the same speed-ups; the result comes from steering, timing and endurance. A fairness test on 30 race courses found a safe line through every one, even at the densest stretch.
  • Timing checks. A rider is ruled out if inputs stop for 3 seconds, if inputs arrive more than 1 second ahead of the server's clock (a sped-up game), or if the input stream does not line up.
  • Bot detection. When a race ends, the server compares each winner's ride with the game's built-in bot on the same slope. If the win follows the bot (for example, steering almost exactly like it and jumping on its cue), the race is voided and everyone is refunded. There is no back office; the server decides on its own. Only the race winner is checked.
  • One session per account. Each account has one connection; signing in from a second tab or device takes over and disconnects the first ("signed in elsewhere").
  • Real looks only. The server reads each rider's outfit from their profile, so nobody can show items they do not own.
Known limits of the anti-cheat
  • A bot written from scratch (not the game's own) is not detected.
  • Very short wins (under about 10 seconds of steering) cannot be judged.
  • The bot check was calibrated on only three human rides, all from one person; the notes say to collect more real races before real stakes.
